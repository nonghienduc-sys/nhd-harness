# VietnamBusinessInsider-inspired macro/commodity style guide

This is a synthesis of recurring high-level editorial patterns observed in public VietnamBusinessInsider macro and commodity articles in 2025–2026. It is not a repository of source prose and must not be used to reproduce article wording.

## Observed patterns

### 1. Lead architecture
- Start with a current market event, macro condition, policy shift or hard data point.
- Establish a tension within the next paragraph: the visible headline is not the whole story.
- Typical logic: fact -> apparent interpretation -> deeper contradiction.

### 2. Paragraph rhythm
- Analytical paragraphs are usually 2–5 sentences.
- The prose is continuous and editorial, not fragmented into many one-line statements.
- Short standalone lines are used sparingly to mark a pivot.

### 3. Heading behavior
- Headings often carry the argument themselves rather than merely naming the topic.
- They are direct and concrete: supply, inflation, USD, structural risk, investment implication.
- Uppercase may appear, but for Word/forum use sentence case is cleaner unless the user explicitly requests uppercase.

### 4. Data placement
- Numbers appear near the beginning and throughout the article.
- Data is followed by explanation, comparison or causal interpretation.
- Market price, volume, percentage change, inventory, trade flow and historical benchmark are preferred evidence types.

### 5. Rhetorical devices
- Strong use of contrasts such as:
  - apparent strength vs hidden fragility;
  - cyclical vs structural;
  - price signal vs physical market;
  - short-term reaction vs long-term implication;
  - efficiency vs resilience;
  - headline number vs underlying composition.
- Common transitions at the conceptual level: "Thoạt nhìn", "Tuy nhiên", "Điều đáng chú ý", "Câu chuyện thực sự", "Trong khi đó", "Đối với nhà đầu tư".

### 6. Investment framing
- After describing the macro event, explain what changes for investors or market participants.
- Translate the story into variables: spreads, inventories, yields, USD, inflation expectations, margins, physical flows, positioning.
- Avoid simplistic buy/sell calls unless supported by a clearly stated scenario and risk condition.

### 7. Ending
- Conclusions are usually cautious.
- Prefer readiness and monitoring over prediction.
- End with the variable or evidence that will determine whether the thesis is confirmed.

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- Generic "Tóm tắt nhanh" boxes by default.
- Excessive bullet lists in analytical sections.
- Marketing language, sensational promises, guaranteed returns.
- Repeated one-sentence paragraphs that make the article feel machine-generated.
- Jargon without economic explanation.
- Restating the same number several times without adding a new interpretation.
- Copying memorable language, metaphors or sentence structure from the reference site.

## Article QA checklist
- [ ] Concrete market fact in first 120 words.
- [ ] Central contradiction/tension clear by paragraph 3.
- [ ] 4–7 analytical sections.
- [ ] Every section contains data/mechanism/implication where relevant.
- [ ] English trading terms are limited and explained.
- [ ] At least one invalidation risk or counter-scenario.
- [ ] Conclusion states what to monitor next.
- [ ] Sources preserved and factual claims traceable.
- [ ] No close imitation of source phrasing.
