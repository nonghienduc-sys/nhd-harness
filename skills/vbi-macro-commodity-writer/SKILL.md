# VBI Macro & Commodity Writer

## Purpose

Use this skill to draft or rewrite Vietnamese market commentary so it reads like a serious finance/business editorial rather than a generic blog, checklist, sales post or AI summary.

This skill is inspired by recurring structural patterns observed in current VietnamBusinessInsider macro/commodity articles. It captures high-level editorial techniques only. Never imitate or reproduce distinctive passages, sentences, metaphors, promotional blocks, author signatures, phone numbers, or article-specific wording.

## Core editorial DNA

1. **Open with the market fact, then reveal the tension.**
   - First 1–2 paragraphs should establish the event, price move, economic data or policy shift.
   - Introduce the contradiction immediately: what the headline appears to mean versus what the underlying structure may mean.
   - Prefer: "Thoạt nhìn... Tuy nhiên...", "Điều đáng chú ý không chỉ nằm ở...", "Câu chuyện thực sự nằm ở...".
   - Avoid long scene-setting, generic definitions and throat-clearing.

2. **Move from data to mechanism to investment implication.**
   Each major section should answer three questions in sequence:
   - What changed? Give the relevant numbers, flows or policy facts.
   - Why does it matter economically? Explain the transmission mechanism.
   - What should a market participant watch next? Translate it into pricing, spreads, risk premia, positioning or scenarios.

3. **Use paragraphs as analytical units, not one-line fragments.**
   - Default paragraph length: 2–5 sentences.
   - One-sentence paragraphs are allowed only for a genuine pivot or conclusion, not every thought.
   - Avoid excessive bullet lists. Lists are reserved for compact data, scenarios or a short monitoring checklist.

4. **Headings should state an argument.**
   Good headings are thesis-like and directional, e.g.:
   - "Nguồn cung tăng, nhưng chưa chắc là nguồn cung mới"
   - "Điểm nghẽn nằm ở sản phẩm lọc dầu, không chỉ ở dầu thô"
   - "Crack spread mới là nơi thị trường bộc lộ niềm tin"
   Avoid generic headings such as "Phân tích", "Tác động", "Kết luận" unless needed for formal structure.

5. **Keep the prose expert but readable.**
   - Vietnamese is primary.
   - Use English market terms only when they are standard and analytically useful: crack spread, front spread, backwardation, risk premium, refinery, outright, long/short.
   - Explain the term naturally on first use if the audience may not know it.
   - Do not overuse jargon or English for decoration.

6. **Use contrast as the main rhetorical engine.**
   Frequent structural moves:
   - headline vs underlying reality;
   - nominal supply vs incremental supply;
   - crude vs refined products;
   - short-term price reaction vs medium-term physical balance;
   - policy announcement vs actual flows;
   - optimism vs fragility;
   - cyclical factor vs structural constraint.

7. **Numbers should appear early and do analytical work.**
   - Put the most decision-relevant number in the opening 2–4 paragraphs.
   - Compare it with a benchmark, previous level, physical equivalent or market scale.
   - Never leave a number isolated; explain what it changes in the economic story.

8. **Avoid over-formatting.**
   - No large "TÓM TẮT NHANH" box unless the user explicitly asks for one.
   - No decorative quote boxes by default.
   - No excessive bolding; bold only 3–6 truly important claims in a long Word article.
   - Prefer clean headings and continuous prose, similar to a business publication page.

9. **Build the article around 4–7 analytical sections.**
   Suggested order for macro/commodity news:
   1. Event + immediate market reaction.
   2. Hidden supply/demand or policy mechanism.
   3. The market variable that matters most (spread, inventory, FX, rates, freight, etc.).
   4. Macro transmission (inflation, growth, central bank, trade balance, corporate margins).
   5. Counter-risk / what could invalidate the thesis.
   6. Scenarios or variables to monitor.
   7. Cautious conclusion.

10. **End with positioning, not prophecy.**
    - Do not claim certainty.
    - State what evidence would confirm or falsify the view.
    - A strong final paragraph often says the market may react to the announcement first, but the medium-term trend depends on physical flows, policy execution or economic data.


## Formal register (default)

Unless the user explicitly asks for a conversational, social-media, or highly promotional tone, use a **formal editorial register** suitable for a professional economics/finance publication.

- Prefer complete, balanced sentences and precise economic terminology.
- Avoid colloquial or sensational phrasing such as "nghe rất lớn", "câu chuyện thực sự là", "con voi trong căn phòng", "cú xoay", "cú sốc" when a more neutral formulation is available.
- Avoid direct rhetorical questions in titles and headings unless they materially improve clarity.
- Avoid unnecessary English adjectives such as "bearish/bullish". Prefer "gây áp lực giảm giá", "hỗ trợ xu hướng tăng", "làm thu hẹp chênh lệch". Keep English only for established technical terms such as crack spread, backwardation, front spread, WTI and Brent, and explain them on first use.
- Prefer "cho thấy", "hàm ý", "phản ánh", "có thể dẫn tới", "đặt ra rủi ro", "cần được đánh giá trong bối cảnh" over dramatic verbs or market slang.
- Maintain analytical distance. Avoid sounding as though the writer is urging the reader to trade immediately.
- Use transitions such as "Tuy nhiên", "Đáng lưu ý", "Xét trên phương diện", "Trong bối cảnh này", "Từ góc độ định giá", "Về trung hạn", "Ngược lại".
- Titles should read like professional research/editorial headlines: factual subject + analytical implication.
- Headings should be concise propositions, not clickbait questions.
- Default conclusion: synthesize evidence, state uncertainty, and identify the indicators that would confirm or invalidate the assessment.

### Formality QA

Before finalizing, check that:
- no colloquial metaphor remains unless indispensable;
- no excessive rhetorical question remains;
- English market slang has been translated or explained;
- claims are calibrated with words such as "có thể", "nhiều khả năng", "phụ thuộc", "chưa đủ cơ sở" where uncertainty exists;
- paragraph transitions are explicit and professional;
- tone resembles an economics/markets commentary prepared for an informed readership, not a personal blog post.

## Preferred sentence patterns

Use these as structural templates, not fixed copy:

- "Thoạt nhìn, ... Tuy nhiên, phía sau con số này là một vấn đề lớn hơn: ..."
- "Điều đáng chú ý không chỉ nằm ở X, mà ở cách X làm thay đổi Y."
- "Câu chuyện thực sự của thị trường lúc này không phải là X, mà là Y."
- "Nếu nhìn từ góc độ định giá, ..."
- "Đối với nhà đầu tư, biến số cần theo dõi không nằm ở tiêu đề mà ở ..."
- "Điều này tạo ra hai lực tác động trái chiều."
- "Kịch bản này chỉ được xác nhận khi ..."

Do not repeat these patterns mechanically; vary phrasing and cadence.

## Rewrite workflow

When rewriting an existing article:

1. Extract a fact table first: event, date, actors, quantities, prices, percentage moves, policy details, uncertainties, sources.
2. Identify the article's single central tension in one sentence.
3. Remove blog-like scaffolding: redundant summary boxes, repeated bullet lists, generic definitions, excessive callouts.
4. Rebuild the opening around the strongest data point and tension.
5. Group the body into mechanism-driven sections.
6. Insert market implications only where supported by a clear causal chain.
7. Add one explicit counterargument or failure condition.
8. End with 3–5 variables that would confirm/invalidate the thesis, preferably in prose; use a short list only if it improves scanning.
9. Verify that factual claims remain unchanged unless fresher verified data is available.
10. Run a style QA against `references/style-guide.md`.

## Word/forum formatting defaults

For Word documents intended for web/forum publication:

- A4, 1.8–2.0 cm margins.
- Body: Aptos/Calibri 11–11.5 pt, line spacing 1.15, 5–7 pt after paragraphs.
- Title: 18–20 pt bold; sentence/title case rather than full caps unless the user wants a tabloid look.
- Heading 1: 14–15 pt bold, dark blue/black; Heading 2: 12–13 pt bold.
- Do not justify if it creates large word gaps; left alignment is acceptable for web-first readability.
- No first-line indent for web articles.
- Keep paragraphs compact and visually continuous.
- Put sources at the end under "Nguồn tham khảo"; hyperlinks should remain clickable.
- Include a concise disclaimer for investment content when appropriate.

## Quality gate

A draft is not finished until all are true:

- Opening contains a concrete fact/number within the first 120 words.
- The central tension is clear by the third paragraph.
- Each heading expresses an analytical proposition.
- The article contains at least one mechanism chain (e.g. diesel → logistics → inflation → monetary policy).
- The article distinguishes announcement/expectation from realized physical data.
- At least one counter-scenario is included.
- No unsupported trading instruction is presented as certainty.
- No copied passage or close paraphrase from VietnamBusinessInsider or any other source.
- The conclusion identifies what data should be watched next.
